# Phase 11 - Candidate Generation and Surrogate Ranking

## Scope and contract

Phase 11 evaluates the 4,000 candidates already generated by Phase 10. It
does not retrain the generator, perform a new inverse-design fit, or claim
independent electromagnetic validation. The selected Phase 7B CNN remains a
learned forward screening surrogate.

Each target has eight generated binary geometries. Candidates are grouped by
target, filtered, deduplicated by exact binary geometry, and ranked by the
screened response MSE. Three policies are reported:

1. `all`: rank every generated candidate.
2. `valid`: retain only candidates passing the deterministic geometry-validity
   checks from Phase 10.
3. `valid_novel`: additionally require pixel Hamming novelty of at least 0.05
   and Phase 8 latent novelty MSE of at least 0.25 relative to the training
   geometry set.

The response-success threshold is 0.30, matching the Phase 10 evaluation
contract and remaining close to the train-only nearest-neighbor screening
baseline.

## Aggregate results

| Policy | target coverage | best screened response MSE | success fraction | selected validity | pixel novelty | latent novelty MSE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| all | 1.000 | 0.338217 | 0.634 | 0.984 | 0.160977 | 0.723211 |
| valid | 1.000 | 0.338230 | 0.634 | 1.000 | 0.161492 | 0.723560 |
| valid_novel | 0.728 | 0.451252 | 0.508 | 1.000 | 0.212665 | 0.943453 |

The validity filter removes very few useful candidates: it raises selected
validity to 1.0 while changing the mean best screened response MSE by only
0.000013. The novelty filter produces more distinct-from-training designs,
but loses 13.6 percentage points of target coverage and worsens the mean best
screened response MSE by 0.113021.

The all-policy ranking retains 500/500 target coverage and removes only four
exact duplicate geometries across the per-target candidate sets. The
valid_novel policy retains 364/500 targets, with an average of 4.974 candidates
remaining per target. Its loss of coverage is therefore a real feasibility
tradeoff, not merely a change in ranking order.

## Complexity-stratified results

| Policy / group | targets covered | best screened response MSE | success fraction | pixel novelty | latent novelty MSE |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| all / simple | 178/178 | 0.124743 | 0.888 | 0.045931 | 0.225409 |
| all / medium | 155/155 | 0.284115 | 0.710 | 0.144657 | 0.732305 |
| all / complex | 167/167 | 0.615966 | 0.293 | 0.298746 | 1.245362 |
| valid_novel / simple | 74/178 | 0.235344 | 0.784 | 0.072424 | 0.341435 |
| valid_novel / medium | 124/155 | 0.354590 | 0.637 | 0.178144 | 0.888460 |
| valid_novel / complex | 166/167 | 0.619705 | 0.289 | 0.300970 | 1.252903 |

Complex targets remain the dominant response-matching bottleneck. They have
the highest novelty and nearly complete novelty-filter coverage, but only
0.293 success under the unconstrained ranking. Simple targets show the
opposite pattern: they are easy to screen accurately but are often close to
the training distribution, so the novelty policy removes many of them.

## Scientific decision

**Supported as a surrogate-ranking stage, with validity-first ranking as the
default artifact-level policy.** The `valid` policy preserves full coverage,
ensures every selected top candidate passes deterministic geometry checks, and
has effectively the same learned-screening error as ranking all candidates.

The `valid_novel` policy is appropriate only when novelty is a hard design
requirement. It should not be presented as a general improvement because it
reduces coverage and response satisfaction. None of these rankings provides
Maxwell or RCWA evidence; the next physically meaningful step remains small
independent verification only if a mature solver path is available.
